Overview

Stackday: salary maxxing on Base

Stackday is a payroll and investing app on Base. A share of each salary can buy tokenized stocks, Bitcoin, Ether or gold into a vault that only you control, and when you need cash you can borrow against what you hold instead of selling it.

This is a testnet demo. Everything runs on Base Sepolia, a test network where the money has no value. The stock, Bitcoin, Ether and gold tokens are mock stand-ins with mock prices. Nothing is on Base mainnet, and there has been no independent security audit. Nothing here is investment advice.

In plain words

Your employer pays you in digital dollars (USDC). Before you collect your pay, you can say "put 10% of this into Apple and Bitcoin". That part is bought for you and kept in your own safe, which only your wallet can open. The rest of your pay goes wherever you want.

Later, if you need cash, you do not have to sell. You can borrow against what is in your safe and pay it back when you are ready. No employer? You can do the same thing from your own wallet, once or every week.

Who it is for

Businesses

Fund a USDC treasury once, add your team by their Stackday ID or name, and pay them once or on a rhythm (daily, weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly) that keeps going while the treasury has money.

Team members

Claim each payday to any wallet. Before claiming, send a share of it into assets, once or on every payday, and see holdings, plans and loans in one place.

Anyone, without an employer

The "Personal" account uses the same vault: buy once or set a recurring buy from your own wallet, then borrow against it the same way.

About the project

Stackday is an entry in the Base "Stocks Maxxing" hackathon, Track 3 (programmable stock markets), aimed at stock-backed loans. The deadline is December 5, 2026. It is built by the founder, Cloakie47, with growthmindsetgg contributing. The code is public at github.com/Cloakie47/stackday, and the demo runs at stackday-rho.vercel.app.

What Stackday is not: it runs no lending pool and no pooled fund, it holds nobody's money (each vault belongs to its owner), and it launches no token. Tokenized stocks are meant for eligible people outside the United States and carry no voting rights.

Getting started

Connect a browser wallet on Base Sepolia, get free test money, prove the wallet is yours with a free signature, and pick Personal or Business. Or look around first with the demo vault, no wallet needed.

In plain words

You need a crypto wallet in your browser, set to the Base Sepolia test network. Test dollars and test gas are free from public faucets.

When you sign in, your wallet asks you to sign a short message. That signature is free, sends nothing and moves no money. It just proves the wallet is yours. Then you tell Stackday whether you are using it for yourself or for a business.

Look first, without a wallet

Open the demo vault. It is the founder's test wallet shown in read-only mode. Adding ?view=0x... with any address to an app page shows that address's vault, plans and loans with every action turned off.

Set up in five steps

  1. Get a browser wallet

    Any wallet that runs in your browser works (the app connects to the wallet your browser offers). Use a wallet just for testing. Stackday never asks for a private key or recovery phrase.

  2. Get test money

    Get test USDC from the Circle faucet (choose Base Sepolia), and a little Base Sepolia test ETH from any Base Sepolia faucet to pay network fees.

  3. Press Start and connect

    On the app's landing page, press Start and connect your wallet. If it is on another network, the app shows Switch to Base Sepolia.

  4. Prove it is yours

    Sign the message "Sign in to Stackday. This proves you own this wallet. It costs nothing and sends no transaction." This keeps you signed in for 7 days.

  5. Choose Personal or Business

    Personal is for investing on your own. Business asks one more thing: "I run the business" (owner) or "I get paid by a business" (team member). An owner adds a business name and an optional description.

Your profile and Stackday ID

Your first sign-in creates a profile with a unique ID such as SD-7K3F9Q2M (SD, then 8 characters that leave out 0, 1, I and O so they are easy to read aloud). A name is optional. Businesses find team members by this ID, by name or by wallet address, and the person accepts the invite before the business can see their attendance. Friends can send you a gift box with it.

Testnet tools

Edge cases and limits

  • One wallet, two hats. One wallet can be both Personal and Business. The choice sets your default pages and can be switched from the menu.
  • No database, still works. Names, team lists, attendance and gift messages live in an optional account database. Money never depends on it. Without it, your account choice is saved on the device only and those extras switch off.
  • No test stock faucet. You get test stock tokens by buying them with test USDC in the app, not from a faucet.
  • Wallet support. The app connects to the wallet extension your browser offers. The earlier Smart wallet option was removed on November 26, 2026.

Features

Payday and salary investing

A business funds a USDC treasury and pays its team once or on a schedule. Each person claims to any wallet and can send a share of that payday into assets held in their own vault.

Live on testnetMock asset tokens

In plain words

Your employer puts money into a payroll account on the blockchain and sets your pay. When payday comes, you collect it. Before you do, you can say "invest 10% of this", pick what it buys, and that part goes into your own vault. The rest comes to you as normal.

You can do it just for this payday, or save it as a plan for every payday. If a purchase cannot go through, you simply get that part as cash.

How it works for a business

  1. Fund the treasury

    The owner deposits test USDC into the Payroll contract once. Each employer's treasury is kept separate: a payday can only spend its own employer's balance.

  2. Add the team

    Find people by Stackday ID, name or wallet address. They accept the invite. Salaries typed into the team list are stored in the optional account database, not on chain.

  3. Pay once, or start a schedule

    Pay everyone now, or start a schedule: daily, weekly, every 2 weeks or monthly (30 days). A schedule pays from the treasury while it has money. It can be paused and resumed; resuming gives no back pay for the paused time.

A schedule does not pay itself. When a payday is due, someone has to press the button. Anyone may do it (the contract only lets it pay the amounts the employer set), and Stackday's trigger bot presses it on weekdays at 16:00 UTC. Each press pays one payday. If the treasury is short, the payday waits until it is topped up.

How it works for a team member

On the Payday page you see salary waiting to be claimed. Claiming sends it to any wallet you choose. Before you claim, one split picker asks how much to invest:

  • None, 10%, 20% or a custom share of this payday.
  • Spread across up to 6 assets, evenly or adjusted per asset.
  • Just this time (free) or as a plan for every payday (the recurring fee of 0.15% applies to the invested part only; salary paid out is never charged).

What the contract does when you claim

  • The Payroll contract creates your vault the first time you invest (the address is fixed in advance for every wallet).
  • For each asset it buys share x salary worth at the price feed, with price protection, and puts the result into your vault as free (not locked) assets.
  • It records Total invested on chain, which the Portfolio page shows next to what your holdings are worth now.
  • If your emergency rule is on and your loan is close to its limit, the whole share goes into the rule's asset and is locked instead.

Other payroll features

  • Attendance. A simple daily check-in that the owner can look at for the last 14 days. It is a record only and never changes anyone's pay.
  • Past paydays and the treasury balance are on the Payroll page. The owner can withdraw money that is not set aside for a payday.
  • Private pay. Team members can be paid with encrypted amounts through Fairblock. See Private pay.

Edge cases and limits

  • Unclaimed pay. An employer can take back a salary that was never claimed, but only 180 days after that payday. It returns to the treasury.
  • Nothing is locked in advance. Future paydays are not guaranteed. If the treasury is short, a due payday waits.
  • Testnet rhythm. On testnet the shortest schedule interval is 60 seconds, so a schedule can be shown in a demo. On mainnet it would be one day.
  • Up to 200 people per schedule, and up to 6 assets per payday plan.
  • Payday spam. A stranger can send tiny paydays to an address. They cannot take or block anything, but the app lists the latest 60 entries, so heavy spam could hide an older unclaimed salary from the screen (it stays claimable).
  • No cancel button on chain. A schedule is stopped by pausing it.

Features

Your vault

Every person gets their own vault contract. Anyone can put value in; only your wallet can take value out. There is no admin, no pooled money and no upgrade switch.

Live on testnetMock interest venue

In plain words

Your vault is like a personal safe-deposit box that lives on the blockchain. Your payday shares, your buys and gifts from friends all go into it. Only the wallet you signed up with has the key.

Not even the Stackday team can open it or move what is inside. The flip side: if you lose access to your wallet, nobody can recover the vault for you.

How it works

The VaultFactory contract makes one small vault per wallet. The address is worked out in advance from your wallet, so Payroll, SipRouter and GiftBox can all send to the same vault, and the vault is created the first time something needs it. Anyone may trigger that creation, which is harmless: the vault always belongs to the wallet it was made for, its owner is set once and can never change, and it cannot be set up again or taken over.

Free and locked

Free assets are tokens sitting in the vault. Locked assets have been posted to a Morpho market as collateral for a loan. New buys always land free; you lock them yourself on the Borrow page (or the emergency rule locks a payday share if you turned it on).

Withdraw

You can withdraw to any address at any time. If the free balance is not enough, the vault unlocks collateral automatically, as far as the loan allows. Once a loan is repaid, everything can come out.

Earn interest on idle USDC

USDC sitting in your vault can be put to work in an interest vault (a standard ERC-4626 vault, such as a Morpho vault). Earn uses USDC already in the vault first, then takes the rest from your wallet. Stop earning brings it back. Stackday's earn fee is 0.1% a year of the amount earning, taken only out of the interest, never the deposit. On testnet the interest venue is a mock and is labelled as such; a real Morpho vault was tested on a copy of Base mainnet.

What the Portfolio page shows

Holdings (free and locked, with their value), your plans, loans and their health, and Total invested: the lifetime USDC you put in through buys and payday shares, kept on chain by SipRouter and Payroll, next to what it is worth now and the change.

Edge cases and limits

  • No recovery. A vault has one owner key. Losing the wallet loses access to the vault.
  • Issuer powers. The real stock tokens have an issuer who can pause, block or seize tokens. No contract can protect against that.
  • Dividends. Real Coinbase tokens reinvest dividends by raising an on-chain multiplier, so each token is worth more over time, locked collateral included. The Borrow page estimates them after the issuer's 5% fee and up to 30% US withholding tax. The testnet mocks pay none.
  • Invest-only assets can be held and withdrawn but not locked.

Features

Invest: one-time, recurring and themes

Buy an asset once from your own wallet, or set a recurring buy that runs every week. Themes suggest a split across assets from fixed, published rules. No employer needed.

Live on testnetMock tokens and pricesThemes: partly built

In plain words

You can buy a slice of a stock, Bitcoin, Ether or gold whenever you like, and it goes straight into your vault. Or set it and forget it: "buy $10 of NVIDIA every Monday for 12 weeks". Your wallet allows exactly that much and no more, and a bot presses the buy button on time.

Not sure what to pick? Themes let you choose topics you care about, like AI or steady big companies, and suggest how to split your money. You decide whether to use it.

Buy once

Pick an asset and an amount of test USDC. The buy goes through SipRouter, which buys at the price feed and sends the asset into your vault as free. One-time buys have no fee. If the exchange quote is more than 1% worse than the reference price, the app stops with "Price moved more than 1%. Try again." Manual buys work at any hour and show the exchange rate.

Recurring buys

  • Amount: any amount, with quick picks of $5, $10, $25 and $50.
  • How often: weekly, every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks.
  • How long: 4, 12 or 26 weeks, a year, or until you stop it. The length is set by an exact wallet approval: SipRouter can never take more than you approved.
  • When: a weekday you pick, at 11:00 New York time while the US market is open. The plan keeps that slot.
  • Fee: 0.15% of each recurring buy, shown before you sign.
  • One plan per asset. Each plan has Run now (when due), Extend (when finished) and Cancel.

Themes

Themes are the "My themes" tab inside Research. Pick the themes you care about and a risk level, and Stackday suggests a split for your next buys. The split comes from fixed, published rules in code: the same choices always give the same split, and no AI model picks your investments. It is information, not advice, and it does not aim to beat the market.

Themes available: AI and chips, Big tech core, Future movers, Steady core (S&P 500 fund and gold) and Crypto native. Clean energy, Healthcare and Global stocks are marked coming soon. Themes only use tokens that really exist on Base; on testnet only some have a mock token, and the others are shown as a preview and never bought.

Using a split

  • From salary: save it as your every-payday plan (10, 20 or 30% of salary, at most 6 assets).
  • From your wallet: buy it once, or set one recurring buy per asset.
  • Rebalance with new money: new money goes to holdings below target first. Nothing is sold, because Stackday cannot sell for you; a notice says so when a holding drifts above its target.

Market mood

Market mood summarises recent news about the stocks in your themes. It does not predict prices and does not change your amounts. Snapshot, not live On the test site it comes from a snapshot built by hand from public headlines on November 15, 2026. The live weekly news refresh for Themes is not built yet; the separate weekly research report does read live news.

Edge cases and limits

  • A plan needs USDC in your wallet on the day. If there is not enough, that buy is skipped and the plan carries on at the next slot.
  • Missed slots are skipped, not caught up. The next buy stays on the original weekday slot.
  • Stale prices. On mainnet, recurring buys refuse a price older than about 26 hours, because stock prices freeze when markets close. This check is switched off on testnet, where the mock feed only moves when the demo wallet changes it.
  • Contract limits. SipRouter allows at least 1 day between buys, a first buy within 31 days and at most 10% slippage; the app uses 1%.
  • Smallest themed trade on testnet is $0.25.
  • Selling and true rebalancing need a sell path on mainnet (issue #35), planned after the deadline.

Borrow instead of selling

Need cash but want to keep your investments? Lock some of them as a guarantee and borrow test USDC against them through Morpho, an existing lending market on Base.

Live on testnet (Morpho)Mock asset pricesAave not built

In plain words

Think of a pawn shop that never sells your things while you keep up. You hand over some stock as a guarantee, you get cash, and the stock is still yours. When you pay the cash back, you take the stock home.

The one rule: if the stock falls a lot, the guarantee stops covering the loan, and the lender may sell part of it to get its money back. Stackday shows you, before you sign, how far prices can fall before that happens.

How it works

Stackday does not lend money itself and runs no lending pool. Your vault borrows directly from a Morpho market. Each asset you can borrow against has its own market, with that asset as the guarantee (collateral) and USDC as the loan.

Free and locked

Everything you buy lands in your vault as free: it is not backing anything, and you can take it out at any time. To borrow, you first lock some of it. Locked assets are posted to the Morpho market and back your loan. New buys are never locked for you (a founder decision, issue #29). The only thing that locks by itself is the emergency rule further down, and only if you switch it on.

The collateral basket

On the Borrow page you can lock any mix of assets first and see one total and one limit. When you borrow or repay, Stackday splits the amount across the Morpho markets so that every part has the same health. Each asset is still its own market and can be liquidated on its own, and the page says so.

Your limit and the three risk levels

Every market has a liquidation limit (Morpho calls it LLTV): the share of the collateral's value that can be borrowed before the loan can be liquidated.

On the testnet deployment the limits match the main real markets on Base for each kind of asset: 62.5% for Apple, NVIDIA, Meta and SpaceX, 77% for Alphabet, gold and the S&P 500 fund token, and 86% for Bitcoin and Ether. The full list is on Invest.

Borrow asks you to pick how much of that limit to use:

Each level shows the amount it really gives you and the price drop it really survives, and says when the market does not have enough cash to lend.

Your vault also has its own safety cap: by default it refuses any loan above 50% of the limit. Choosing High raises the cap to 65% in the same signature, and only when the amount needs it. The contract never allows more than 85%.

What it costs

Borrowing is shown as one Borrow APY: Morpho's interest, which compounds every second, plus Stackday's fee of 0.1% a year. The fee builds up by the second on the amount you owe. You receive the full amount you borrow; the fee is paid when you repay, before the loan itself. If a loan ends in liquidation instead, the unpaid fee is dropped. See Fees.

Repaying and taking assets out

  • Repay is manual. There is no forced repayment and no schedule. Repay part or all, any time. Paying more than you owe only takes what is owed.
  • Withdraw is one control. It takes free assets first and releases locked ones automatically, but only as much as the loan allows.
  • Anyone can repay into a vault (for example a friend paying for you), but only you can borrow, unlock or withdraw.

The risk screen

The Risk page shows your borrowing cost, limit, health, liquidation price and the price drop that triggers it, the liquidation penalty, the exact Morpho market used and how fresh the price is. It has a stress test for overnight price gaps of 10, 20 and 30% (or your own number), and shows what happens if transfers of the stock token are paused. With no loan you can type an example loan to try it.

The emergency rule

For people paid through Stackday payroll there is an optional safety net. You choose a borrowable asset, a health level (the app offers 1.25, 1.50 or 2.00) and a share of salary. On a payday, if your loan's health is at or below that level when you claim, the payday share switches to that asset and is locked straight away as collateral, so the loan gets safer.

It has been shown on testnet: in the judges' sequence a 30% price drop took a loan's health to 1.45, the next payday's emergency share added collateral, and health went back up to 3.53. The transactions are listed in the repository README.

Edge cases and limits

  • Small testnet markets. Each test Morpho market was funded with about 4 test USDC, so demo loans must be small. Larger borrows are refused until the founder tops the markets up.
  • Invest-only assets cannot be locked. Tesla, Amazon, Microsoft, Strategy, AMD and the Treasuries token have no Morpho market. You can buy, hold and withdraw them, never borrow against them.
  • Prices can jump. Stock prices can gap after a weekend or overnight. A liquidation on Morpho's stock markets costs about 12.7% of the part sold.
  • Paused tokens. The real stock tokens have an issuer who can pause transfers. During a pause you can still repay, but you cannot add or remove collateral. The judges' sequence tested this on testnet.
  • Mock prices on testnet. Loan health uses the mock price feeds, which only move when the demo wallet changes them. Price charts on the Invest page show real market prices for reference only.
  • Aave is not built. The anchor names Morpho and Aave. Only Morpho is built; Aave would plug in behind the same adapter.
  • Dividends. The real Coinbase tokens reinvest dividends by raising an on-chain multiplier, so locked collateral grows too. The testnet mocks pay none.

Research: the weekly report and the daily note

Every Monday, Stackday publishes a research report on the six assets you can also borrow against. Code gathers and computes every number with its source; AI models only interpret, and a person approves the report before anyone sees it.

BuiltBeta: free previewPaywall off

In plain words

Once a week you get a plain-language briefing on Bitcoin, Ether, gold, NVIDIA, Apple and the S&P 500: what happened last week, what the charts and the economy look like, what could happen next, and a general view such as "hold" or "avoid new buys".

It is general research for everyone, never advice for you personally. Every figure points to where it came from, and last week's calls are graded in public, wrong ones included.

What is in the weekly report

For each of the six assets, its own page on Research with these parts:

  1. Last week: the price move and what drove it, with sources.
  2. Chart read on daily, weekly and monthly bars: trend, support and resistance, patterns found by code, and how volatile the asset is against its own history. Each pattern has a confidence level and the price that would cancel it.
  3. Market conditions: rates and the Fed, Treasury yields, the dollar, and asset-specific signals (funding rates and stablecoin supply for crypto, real yields for gold).
  4. Geopolitics and news: dated headlines from reputable outlets and official sources. Company press releases count as one side, never the only source.
  5. Week ahead: Fed meetings, inflation and jobs releases, options expiry.
  6. Scenarios: bull, base and bear cases, with rough probabilities that add up to 100%, the price levels involved and what would trigger each.
  7. This week's view: Accumulate, Hold, Reduce or Avoid new buys, with a conviction level (low, medium or high), a buy zone if any, the price that would cancel the view, and the two or three reasons that matter most.
  8. Borrowing note: what a large drop would mean for someone borrowing against this asset, for example how a 20% fall moves a loan at half its limit, with a reminder that the emergency rule exists.
  9. All facts used and the asset's scorecard.

Then one page for the whole report: this week's ideas (a short ranked list across the six, written as general example splits for a cautious and a growth-minded reader, never orders for a person) and last week's scorecard.

How a report is made

The structure follows the published TradingAgents research design (analyst roles, then a bull and bear debate, then a risk review). Stackday took the idea only and wrote its own code. The daily note uses a short version: one triage pass on a cheaper model, a stronger model for big movers or answers that fail checks, then the risk check.

How scores work

  • Direction: each daily note is checked after 1 and 5 trading days. Did the price move the way the view said?
  • Levels: did support and resistance hold or break as described?
  • Calibration: over weeks, do high-conviction calls come true more often than low-conviction ones? Shown per conviction level.
  • Facts: the share of claims with a valid source. The target is 100%.
  • Usefulness: the reviewers grade each note from 1 to 5.

The weekly scorecard marks every view from last week as Right, Wrong, Mixed or No data. Wrong calls stay visible. The report becomes a paid product only after four Mondays in a row with every fact sourced, no corrections, an average grade of 4 or more and calibration that is not clearly off, and after a legal check. Until then it is a free preview and the paywall is off.

Gift boxes

Send someone a box worth a chosen amount of test USDC, holding one stock, your own mix, a surprise mix or a research pick. They open it, and the assets land in their own vault.

Live on testnetMock tokens

In plain words

It is like a gift card that turns into stocks. You put in, say, $5 and choose what is inside. Your friend gets a box to open. When they open it, the $5 buys the stocks at that day's price and puts them in their vault, with a little unboxing moment.

If they say no thanks, or nobody opens it before it expires, your money comes back to you. You can also take it back yourself while it is still unopened.

Sending a gift

On Gifts, choose Send a gift:

  • To: a Stackday ID, an @name or a wallet address.
  • Amount: 1 to 500 test USDC. There is no fee on gifts.
  • Box:
    • One stock: 100% of one asset. Any supported asset works, invest-only ones included.
    • My mix: 1 to 6 assets in whole percents, at least 1% each.
    • Surprise: 2 to 4 random assets, each at least 10%, picked in your browser when you send. The contents are hidden in the app until opened. That is cosmetic: the blockchain is public.
    • Research pick: the mix is chosen when the box is opened, from the latest approved weekly report, so it follows the newest research. It uses only the six researched assets.
  • Message (optional): up to 140 characters, kept off chain. Only the sender and the recipient can read it.
  • Expiry: 7, 30 or 90 days.

Sending is one flow in your wallet: approve the USDC, then create the box. The recipient sees a waiting count on the Received tab, a card on Home and a badge on the Gifts menu entry.

How it works

The GiftBox contract holds your USDC until the box is opened. It never holds stock. When the recipient opens it, the contract buys each part of the mix with the gift's USDC and sends it straight into their vault as free assets. This design means a $5 box is always $5 of stock on the day it is opened, a research pick can follow the newest report, and every refund returns exactly the USDC you sent.

Opening a box

  1. The recipient presses Open and signs. For a research pick, the app first builds the mix from the approved report and shows it, labelled "picked by research", before signing.

  2. While the wallet and the transaction are pending, the box gently wiggles.

  3. Only once the transaction is confirmed does the lid pop, confetti burst out and one card per asset rise from the box, each with the amount taken from the transaction receipt.

  4. If it fails, the box closes again with the reason and a Retry. With reduced motion switched on, there is no confetti or movement: the box fades open instead.

Each part is bought at the price feed with slippage protection (the contract allows at most 3%). If any part cannot be bought, the whole opening is undone and the box stays closed, so the sender can still take it back.

Gifts from the chat

You can ask the chat assistant to "send Maya $5 of stocks". It checks the amount and box type, then opens the Gifts page on Send a gift with those filled in. The recipient is always chosen on the Gifts page, never put in a link.

Edge cases and limits

  • No sending to yourself, to the zero address or to the contract. Expiry must be between 1 and 365 days in the contract; the app offers 7, 30 or 90.
  • A recipient with a loan: gifted assets arrive free, never locked, so they do not change the loan.
  • A recipient with no vault: opening creates it.
  • Research pick with no approved report: the box cannot be opened until a report is approved. The contract checks that the mix is valid, but it cannot check the research itself; the app shows the mix before the recipient signs.
  • Messages need the optional account database and its gifts table. The founder adds that table to the live database.
  • Who may receive. The app notes that tokenized stocks are only for people in eligible places outside the US, and that gifts may have tax rules where you live.
  • Mainnet note. The S&P 500 token's price feed is restricted on mainnet, so gifting it there needs another price adapter first.

How one turn works

The card and the pre-check

A card shows the action, the asset, the amounts, the Stackday fee (or None), "to: your wallet" with the full address and a testnet label. Borrow cards add the health after and the liquidation price with the price fall that triggers it. The sentence above a card is written by code from the intent, never by the model.

When a card appears, every call on it is simulated in order on Base Sepolia from your connected wallet, in one request. The card then says one of three things:

ResultWhat you seeConfirm
PassesA green line: it passes, prices can still moveOn
BlockedThe failing step and the reason in plain words, a hint and Check againOff
Could not pre-check"Could not pre-check right now"On

A card expires after 2 minutes. An expired card offers Refresh numbers: the same stored intent is rebuilt through the same builder with fresh chain reads, a fresh expiry and a new pre-check, without asking the model again. Only one card is pending at a time; a new one replaces the old.

Guardrails

  • No keys, no signing. The model only outputs intents. Your wallet shows its own prompt for every step.
  • No receiver field. No intent has an address or "to" field, and an unknown field makes the whole intent fail. Withdrawals and borrows always go to your connected wallet.
  • Caps. At most 500 test USDC per card. A borrow is refused if health afterwards would be below your chosen risk level.
  • Allowlist. Cards may only call USDC, Stackday's contracts, the mock sell desk, the listed asset tokens and your own vault.
  • Untrusted text. Research text reaches the model inside an untrusted block, and no action is accepted in a turn that included it. Later turns see only a stub of that answer.
  • No personal advice. "What should I buy?" gets the general weekly views with the disclaimer and an explanation of the options.
  • Sign-in and limits. The chat needs the wallet sign-in. Each wallet gets 10 messages a minute and 150 a day; if the limit store cannot be reached, the chat refuses rather than running unlimited.
  • It never asks for a private key or recovery phrase.

Receipts and Activity

A finished action leaves a receipt with every transaction link. Receipts are listed under Activity, stored on your device only, and are never sent to the model. Chat history is also kept on the device (the last 40 messages, plus up to 100 older receipts); Clear keeps the receipts.

The dock

The chat opens as a dialog: focus stays inside it and returns to the Ask button when it closes, Esc closes it (except while a wallet signature is running), and new messages are announced politely to screen readers. On phones it is a bottom sheet with a drag handle and a dimmed backdrop. Suggestion chips come from a fixed list in code: by page when the dock is empty, then by card type or by what was asked. A sell card offers "Or borrow X USDC instead" from its own numbers, worded as an option.

Edge cases and limits

  • Small testnet pools. Each Morpho market holds about 4 test USDC to lend and the sell desk holds about 5 test USDC, so larger borrows and sells are refused until the founder tops them up.
  • Selling is testnet only. On mainnet a sell needs a sell adapter (issue #35), planned after the deadline.
  • Gift cards are not pre-checked, because they only open the Gifts page.
  • Mistakes happen. The model can misunderstand. That is why the card, not the chat text, is what you check, and why nothing happens without your signature.
  • No model key, no chat. Without the server's model key the chat route answers that it is unavailable; the rest of the app is unaffected.

Private pay

Salaries on a public blockchain are visible to anyone. Private pay uses Fairblock confidential transfers so that payday amounts are encrypted on chain, while the rest of Stackday works as before.

Phase 1 live on testnet (real, not mocked)Inside Payroll: not built

In plain words

Normally anyone can look up how much a wallet was paid. With private pay, the amount is scrambled so that only you and your employer can read it. People can still see that a payment happened, just not how much.

You collect it with the same split as normal pay: invest a share, keep the rest, and you can leave the rest private.

How it works

Private pay runs beside the Payroll contract, through Fairblock's StableTrust contract on Base Sepolia (0xb6cdAE7ccfEE03e351694c63436D5c5c073aEF84, also live on Base mainnet). Amounts are encrypted; wallet addresses stay visible. The keys come from one wallet signature per visit and stay in your browser.

For the owner

  • Team members turn on private pay from the team invite. On Payroll, Pay the team (Once, now) lists everyone with a Private tick box: ticked for people who are ready, locked to normal pay for anyone not set up.
  • A private payroll balance with Unlock, Top up and Withdraw leftover, so it can be funded before payday, and an estimate of how many signatures a payday needs.
  • A payday log on the device: one entry per payday that opens to show every payment, private or normal, with its transaction, and a CSV export for an accountant or auditor.

For a team member

  • On My pay, collect and invest 0, 10, 20% or a custom share in one flow. The invested part goes in as one-time buys.
  • A saved split for every private payday (percentages only, kept on the device). You still press Collect, because only your browser holds the keys.
  • A reminder on Home and My pay when new private pay has arrived, read from the encrypted balance with no signature and no amount shown.

  • Slow steps. Amounts move in whole cents, and Fairblock steps can take minutes. An end-to-end private payday script took 57 seconds.
  • No one-click payday yet. That needs Fairblock's Fairycloak relay (access on request from Fairblock).
  • Not built: confidential salaries inside the Payroll contract itself, and unlinkable withdrawals.
  • Disclosure. Fairblock's SDK has no auditor keys; scoped disclosure is Fairblock's separate Compliance Dashboard.
  • Device-bound. Logs and saved splits live in the browser that made them.

Fees

Three small fees, 0.2% at most, each fixed in the contracts when they were deployed. Nobody can change them later, and none is ever taken from your salary, your vault or your collateral.

In plain words

Buying once is free. A recurring buy costs 0.15% of what it buys: 15 cents on $100. A loan costs 0.1% a year on top of the lender's interest: about $1 on $1,000 borrowed for a year. Earning interest costs 0.1% a year, paid only out of the interest you earn.

Gifts, withdrawals and collecting your pay are free (you only pay the network's own small gas fee).

Security, risks and what is mocked

What is real on the test network, what is a stand-in, how the app is protected, and what can still go wrong. Read this before trusting any of it.

In plain words

Stackday's own contracts are real and have no admin key that can touch your money. The stocks, Bitcoin, Ether and gold on the test network are pretend versions with prices the demo wallet can move, so the team can show what happens in a crash.

This is a hackathon demo. Nobody outside the team has audited it. Do not use it with real money.

Real and mocked, layer by layer

What stands in for what

  • Asset tokens: mock tokens stand in for Coinbase tokenized stocks (AAPLc, NVDAc and others), cbBTC, WETH, PAXG and the deSPXA and wtSGOV fund tokens. The same code is tested with the real AAPLc token, a real Aerodrome pool and the real Morpho market on a private copy of Base mainnet. Bitcoin and Ether have no mainnet test yet.
  • Price feeds: mock feeds, moved by the demo wallet. Price charts in the app show real market prices, for reference only.
  • The exchange: a mock that mints the asset at exactly the feed price. On mainnet, buys go through Aerodrome Slipstream pools.
  • The sell desk: a mock that buys assets back at the feed price minus 0.5%, from a small test USDC reserve. Used only by the chat's sell card.
  • The interest venue: a plain mock interest vault. A real Morpho vault was tested on a copy of mainnet.
  • Market mood in Themes: a hand-built snapshot, labelled "not live".

How the contracts protect you

  • One vault per person. No contract pools money across people.
  • No admin or owner function can move money out of a vault. Only the vault's owner can withdraw, borrow, unlock or earn.
  • The vault cannot be set up twice or taken over. Creating someone else's vault first is harmless.
  • Adapters act only on the caller's own position and hold nothing between calls.
  • Employer treasuries are separate. A paused schedule pays nothing.
  • A failed purchase never blocks a salary. Over-repaying only takes the debt.
  • Fees are fixed and capped. Reentrancy guards and checks on every external call; tests on every money path (69 local tests, plus fork tests on a copy of Base mainnet).

How the app protects you

  • Sign-in is a free wallet signature (Sign-In with Ethereum) with a single-use code that expires in 10 minutes, tied to the site and the chain. It cannot be replayed.
  • Rate limits on every API route, counted per visitor, plus per-wallet limits on the chat.
  • Security headers and an origin check on requests that change data.
  • The database has row level security on with no public policies: browsers cannot read or write it directly. Only the server can, after checking the signature.
  • Consent: a business only sees a person's attendance after that person accepts the team invite.
  • Every transaction is simulated before your wallet is asked to sign it, with extra gas margin for Morpho.

Risks you should know

No independent audit. The review so far is the builders' own. Do not use with real money.

  • Prices can fall a lot. Every one of the original testnet assets lost money in 2022, from about 26% (Apple) to 68% (Ether). Tokenized assets add smart-contract, issuer, custody and liquidity risk.
  • Liquidation. A loan whose health falls below 1.00 can be liquidated. The emergency rule only acts when a salary is claimed.
  • Issuer powers. The real stock tokens have an issuer who can pause, block or seize tokens. During a pause a loan cannot be topped up, only repaid.
  • Key loss. A vault has one owner key and no recovery.
  • Outside dependencies. Morpho, the interest venue, USDC and the price feeds are code Stackday depends on but does not control.
  • Paydays wait for a button. A due payday or recurring buy waits until someone (usually the trigger bot) runs it.
  • The database is a normal web service. Names, team lists (including salaries the employer typed in), attendance and gift messages are only as private as that database and its keys.
  • Who may use the tokens. Tokenized stocks are for eligible people outside the United States and carry no voting rights. The app gives information, not investment advice.

Testnet-only behaviour

  • Shortest payroll rhythm: 60 seconds (one day on mainnet).
  • The stale price check on recurring buys is off (about 26 hours on mainnet).
  • Each Morpho market holds about 4 test USDC to lend; the sell desk about 5.
  • Selling exists only through the mock sell desk.

For developers

Trigger bot and API routes

A small bot presses the buttons the contracts already let anyone press: due paydays, due recurring buys and expired gift refunds. The API routes handle sign-in, accounts, chat, gifts and research, and the site keeps working when they are switched off.

In plain words

Blockchains do not act on their own: someone has to send a transaction to make a scheduled payday or a weekly buy happen. Stackday's trigger bot does that once each weekday. It cannot change what you set up and it holds none of your money; its wallet only pays the network fee.

The trigger bot

GET /api/keeper runs on a Vercel cron at 16:00 UTC, Monday to Friday (0 16 * * 1-5), which is 11:00 New York time in winter, matching the slot recurring buys are set to.

  • Secrets (names only): CRON_SECRET (at least 16 characters, compared in constant time), KEEPER_PRIVATE_KEY (a gas-only wallet), optional KEEPER_RPC_URL. Recurring buys also need the database settings and the recurring_plans table.
  • Registering a plan: saving a recurring buy calls POST /api/keeper/register with the wallet and asset. No sign-in is needed, because the route checks that the plan really exists on chain.
  • Only the founder's Vercel project runs this cron.

Sign-in

  1. The browser asks POST /api/session/nonce for a single-use code (16 random bytes, stored for 10 minutes).

  2. The wallet signs a Sign-In with Ethereum (EIP-4361) message with the site domain, chain 84532, the code and a 10-minute expiry.

  3. POST /api/session checks the domain, chain and times, deletes the code in one statement (so it can never be used twice), then verifies the signature. Smart contract wallets work too.

  4. The first sign-in creates the profile. The session is an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie holding the wallet and an expiry, signed with HMAC, valid for 7 days.

Research engine

How the daily note and the Monday report are produced: which models run, how a weekly report fits into Vercel's time limits, how reviewers approve it, and what stops a bad run.

In plain words

A computer program collects market data and works out every number. AI models then write about those numbers, other passes check their work, and a person signs off before it is published. If anything goes wrong or gets too expensive, the run stops and publishes nothing.

Models and switches

Switches in web/lib/research/flags.ts, all defaulting to the safe value: RESEARCH_ENGINE_ENABLED plus ANTHROPIC_API_KEY to run with real model calls (otherwise the routes serve the labelled sample), RESEARCH_PAYWALL_ENABLED (off), and spend caps RESEARCH_MAX_RUN_COST_USD ($2), RESEARCH_MAX_WEEKLY_RUN_COST_USD ($4) and RESEARCH_MAX_DAY_COST_USD ($6). Reviewers are the wallets in RESEARCH_REVIEWERS, signed in with the normal wallet sign-in.

The Monday run, step by step

A full weekly report is too long for one serverless call (Vercel Hobby stops a function at 300 seconds), so the cron calls the weekly route once an hour and each call does one step.

The daily run

  1. If the market was closed, carry the last note forward.
  2. Gather data in code, then a triage pass on the cheaper model.
  3. Big movers and answers that fail checks get a second pass on the stronger model.
  4. A risk check in code removes unsupported claims and caps conviction. Buy zone and invalidation levels are computed in code.
  5. If more than two assets end with no reading, nothing is written.

The score job at 23:00 UTC grades notes after 1 and 5 trading days (direction, levels, facts share) and keeps calibration per conviction level. Reviewers add a 1 to 5 grade.

Safety and honesty rules in code

  • Facts first. Every number is a fact with an id, a source, a link and a time (web/lib/research/data). The model never calculates a price, an indicator or a level, and a claim without a fact id is removed.
  • Untrusted text. Headlines, filings and web text go inside an <untrusted_news> block and through an injection screen; anything that reads like instructions to an AI is dropped and logged. Headline links are checked against a whitelist of outlet host names.
  • No money powers. The engine has no wallet, no keys and no tool that sends transactions.
  • Strict JSON. Output is parsed and validated against a schema before rendering.
  • No look-ahead. Bars are cut at the run time and macro series are used only from their real release day, so a report and any backtest only use data that existed then.
  • Reproducible. Model ids, prompt version and a hash of the data are logged per run.
  • Fail closed. A run that fails or hits a cap writes nothing, and can send an alert to RESEARCH_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL.
  • Labels. Every page and report carries the research disclaimer; the weekly report carries the Beta label until the quality bar holds.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers, with links to the page that explains more.

Is this real money?

No. Everything runs on Base Sepolia, a test network. Test USDC is free from the Circle faucet, and the stock, Bitcoin, Ether and gold tokens are mocks. Nothing is on Base mainnet. See Security, risks and mocks.

Can Stackday take my money?

No contract has an admin or owner function that can move money out of your vault. Only your wallet can withdraw, borrow or unlock. The servers hold no key that can move user funds. There has been no independent audit, so this is the builders' own claim, backed by tests. See Your vault.

What happens if I lose my wallet?

You lose access to your vault. There is no recovery, because nobody else holds a key to it.

Do I need an employer?

No. A Personal account uses the same vault with one-time or recurring buys from your own wallet. See Invest.

Is investing part of my salary required?

No. The share is always optional: none, 10%, 20% or your own number, once or every payday. See Payday.

Why borrow instead of selling?

Selling ends your investment (and may be a taxable event where you live). Borrowing gives you cash while you keep the assets. The trade-off is interest and the risk of liquidation if prices fall far. See Borrow instead of selling.

Will I be forced to repay?

No. There is no repayment schedule. The only risk is liquidation if your loan's health falls below 1.00, which the Borrow and Risk pages show before you sign.

Does Stackday lock my new buys automatically?

No. New buys always land free. You lock them yourself. The only automatic lock is the optional emergency rule, and only when a payday is claimed.

What does it cost?

One-time buys, gifts and withdrawals are free. Recurring buys cost 0.15%, loans 0.1% a year on top of Morpho's interest, and earning interest 0.1% a year taken from the interest. See Fees.

Is the research investment advice?

No. It is general research for every reader, with sources for every number and a public scorecard that keeps wrong calls. It never tells a person what to buy or sell. See Research.

Can the chat assistant spend my money?

No. It can only prepare a card. Code builds the card, the receiver is always your own wallet, each card is capped at 500 test USDC, and nothing happens until you confirm and sign in your wallet. See Chat assistant.

Why can I not sell on the Invest page?

The contracts can only buy today. On testnet the chat can sell through a mock sell desk. Selling on mainnet needs a sell adapter (issue #35), planned after the deadline.

What happens to a gift nobody opens?

After it expires (7, 30 or 90 days), anyone can trigger the refund, and the trigger bot does it on its weekday run. The USDC goes back to the sender only. See Gift boxes.

Why did my recurring buy not run?

Common reasons: it was not due yet, your wallet did not have enough USDC that day, or the approval you gave is used up (the plan finished; use Extend). The trigger bot runs on weekdays at 16:00 UTC, and you can press Run now when a buy is due.

Why is my borrow refused?

On testnet each Morpho market holds only about 4 test USDC to lend, so larger loans are refused until the founder tops them up. A borrow is also refused if it would put your loan below the risk level you chose, or above your vault's safety cap.

Who can use tokenized stocks?

Coinbase's tokenized stocks are for eligible people outside the United States and carry no voting rights.

Is Stackday an official Base or Coinbase product?

No. It is an independent hackathon project built on Base.

Where is the code?

At github.com/Cloakie47/stackday. Start with docs/START-HERE.md. See also Architecture and Contracts and addresses.
