Borrow instead of selling

Need cash but want to keep your investments? Lock some of them as a guarantee and borrow test USDC against them through Morpho, an existing lending market on Base.

Think of a pawn shop that never sells your things while you keep up. You hand over some stock as a guarantee, you get cash, and the stock is still yours. When you pay the cash back, you take the stock home.

The one rule: if the stock falls a lot, the guarantee stops covering the loan, and the lender may sell part of it to get its money back. Stackday shows you, before you sign, how far prices can fall before that happens.

How it works

Stackday does not lend money itself and runs no lending pool. Your vault borrows directly from a Morpho market. Each asset you can borrow against has its own market, with that asset as the guarantee (collateral) and USDC as the loan.

Free and locked

Everything you buy lands in your vault as free: it is not backing anything, and you can take it out at any time. To borrow, you first lock some of it. Locked assets are posted to the Morpho market and back your loan. New buys are never locked for you (a founder decision, issue #29). The only thing that locks by itself is the emergency rule further down, and only if you switch it on.

The collateral basket

On the Borrow page you can lock any mix of assets first and see one total and one limit. When you borrow or repay, Stackday splits the amount across the Morpho markets so that every part has the same health. Each asset is still its own market and can be liquidated on its own, and the page says so.

Your limit and the three risk levels

Every market has a liquidation limit (Morpho calls it LLTV): the share of the collateral's value that can be borrowed before the loan can be liquidated.

On the testnet deployment the limits match the main real markets on Base for each kind of asset: 62.5% for Apple, NVIDIA, Meta and SpaceX, 77% for Alphabet, gold and the S&P 500 fund token, and 86% for Bitcoin and Ether. The full list is on Invest.

Borrow asks you to pick how much of that limit to use:

LevelShare of the limitPrice drop it survivesExample: $1,000 of Apple locked
Low30%about 70%borrow $187.50, health 3.33
Mid (the default)50%about 50%borrow $312.50, health 2.00
High65%about 35%borrow $406.25, health 1.54

The example uses Apple's 62.5% limit: $1,000 locked gives a limit of $625. The levels come from broker, bank and Coinbase practice and from the cost of a liquidation on Morpho's stock markets (about 12.7%). Each level shows the amount it really gives you and the price drop it really survives, and says when the market does not have enough cash to lend.

Your vault also has its own safety cap: by default it refuses any loan above 50% of the limit. Choosing High raises the cap to 65% in the same signature, and only when the amount needs it. The contract never allows more than 85%.

What it costs

Borrowing is shown as one Borrow APY: Morpho's interest, which compounds every second, plus Stackday's fee of 0.1% a year. The fee builds up by the second on the amount you owe. You receive the full amount you borrow; the fee is paid when you repay, before the loan itself. If a loan ends in liquidation instead, the unpaid fee is dropped. See Fees.

Repaying and taking assets out

  • Repay is manual. There is no forced repayment and no schedule. Repay part or all, any time. Paying more than you owe only takes what is owed.
  • Withdraw is one control. It takes free assets first and releases locked ones automatically, but only as much as the loan allows.
  • Anyone can repay into a vault (for example a friend paying for you), but only you can borrow, unlock or withdraw.

The risk screen

The Risk page shows your borrowing cost, limit, health, liquidation price and the price drop that triggers it, the liquidation penalty, the exact Morpho market used and how fresh the price is. It has a stress test for overnight price gaps of 10, 20 and 30% (or your own number), and shows what happens if transfers of the stock token are paused. With no loan you can type an example loan to try it.

The emergency rule

For people paid through Stackday payroll there is an optional safety net. You choose a borrowable asset, a health level (the app offers 1.25, 1.50 or 2.00) and a share of salary. On a payday, if your loan's health is at or below that level when you claim, the payday share switches to that asset and is locked straight away as collateral, so the loan gets safer.

It has been shown on testnet: in the judges' sequence a 30% price drop took a loan's health to 1.45, the next payday's emergency share added collateral, and health went back up to 3.53. The transactions are listed in the repository README.

Edge cases and limits

  • Small testnet markets. Each test Morpho market was funded with about 4 test USDC, so demo loans must be small. Larger borrows are refused until the founder tops the markets up.
  • Invest-only assets cannot be locked. Tesla, Amazon, Microsoft, Strategy, AMD and the Treasuries token have no Morpho market. You can buy, hold and withdraw them, never borrow against them.
  • Prices can jump. Stock prices can gap after a weekend or overnight. A liquidation on Morpho's stock markets costs about 12.7% of the part sold.
  • Paused tokens. The real stock tokens have an issuer who can pause transfers. During a pause you can still repay, but you cannot add or remove collateral. The judges' sequence tested this on testnet.
  • Mock prices on testnet. Loan health uses the mock price feeds, which only move when the demo wallet changes them. Price charts on the Invest page show real market prices for reference only.
  • Aave is not built. The anchor names Morpho and Aave. Only Morpho is built; Aave would plug in behind the same adapter.
  • Dividends. The real Coinbase tokens reinvest dividends by raising an on-chain multiplier, so locked collateral grows too. The testnet mocks pay none.
