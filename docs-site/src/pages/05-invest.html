Features

Invest: one-time, recurring and themes

Buy an asset once from your own wallet, or set a recurring buy that runs every week. Themes suggest a split across assets from fixed, published rules. No employer needed.

You can buy a slice of a stock, Bitcoin, Ether or gold whenever you like, and it goes straight into your vault. Or set it and forget it: "buy $10 of NVIDIA every Monday for 12 weeks". Your wallet allows exactly that much and no more, and a bot presses the buy button on time.

Not sure what to pick? Themes let you choose topics you care about, like AI or steady big companies, and suggest how to split your money. You decide whether to use it.

Buy once

Pick an asset and an amount of test USDC. The buy goes through SipRouter, which buys at the price feed and sends the asset into your vault as free. One-time buys have no fee. If the exchange quote is more than 1% worse than the reference price, the app stops with "Price moved more than 1%. Try again." Manual buys work at any hour and show the exchange rate.

Recurring buys

  • Amount: any amount, with quick picks of $5, $10, $25 and $50.
  • How often: weekly, every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks.
  • How long: 4, 12 or 26 weeks, a year, or until you stop it. The length is set by an exact wallet approval: SipRouter can never take more than you approved.
  • When: a weekday you pick, at 11:00 New York time while the US market is open. The plan keeps that slot.
  • Fee: 0.15% of each recurring buy, shown before you sign.
  • One plan per asset. Each plan has Run now (when due), Extend (when finished) and Cancel.

Themes

Themes are the "My themes" tab inside Research. Pick the themes you care about and a risk level, and Stackday suggests a split for your next buys. The split comes from fixed, published rules in code: the same choices always give the same split, and no AI model picks your investments. It is information, not advice, and it does not aim to beat the market.

Themes available: AI and chips, Big tech core, Future movers, Steady core (S&P 500 fund and gold) and Crypto native. Clean energy, Healthcare and Global stocks are marked coming soon. Themes only use tokens that really exist on Base; on testnet only some have a mock token, and the others are shown as a preview and never bought.

Every split keeps targets for new buys: no single stock above 15%, no theme above 40%, the AI and chip makers together at most 35%, each volatile name at most 2.5%. The cash cushion is not bought: on a payday plan it is paid to you, and on a wallet buy it stays in your wallet.

Using a split

  • From salary: save it as your every-payday plan (10, 20 or 30% of salary, at most 6 assets).
  • From your wallet: buy it once, or set one recurring buy per asset.
  • Rebalance with new money: new money goes to holdings below target first. Nothing is sold, because Stackday cannot sell for you; a notice says so when a holding drifts above its target.

Market mood

Market mood summarises recent news about the stocks in your themes. It does not predict prices and does not change your amounts. Snapshot, not live On the test site it comes from a snapshot built by hand from public headlines on November 15, 2026. The live weekly news refresh for Themes is not built yet; the separate weekly research report does read live news.

Edge cases and limits

  • A plan needs USDC in your wallet on the day. If there is not enough, that buy is skipped and the plan carries on at the next slot.
  • Missed slots are skipped, not caught up. The next buy stays on the original weekday slot.
  • Stale prices. On mainnet, recurring buys refuse a price older than about 26 hours, because stock prices freeze when markets close. This check is switched off on testnet, where the mock feed only moves when the demo wallet changes it.
  • Contract limits. SipRouter allows at least 1 day between buys, a first buy within 31 days and at most 10% slippage; the app uses 1%.
  • Smallest themed trade on testnet is $0.25.
  • Selling and true rebalancing need a sell path on mainnet (issue #35), planned after the deadline.
